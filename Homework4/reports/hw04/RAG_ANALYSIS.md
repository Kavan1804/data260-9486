# RAG Analysis

**Retrieval.** With top_k = 3, retrieval found the answering chunk for Q1 (1950.5 chunk #025, "21 calendar days", ranked first) and for Q3 (1946 chunk #002, "30 days' written notice", score 0.73). The 1941.1 refrigerator clause that also mentions "30 days written notice" never reached the top 5, so the cross-document distractor did not cause a problem. Q2 was only half retrieved: the just-cause chunks came back, but the chunk with the relocation amount (#031, "one month of the tenant's rent") ranked fourth, outside k = 3. For Q4 all three chunks came from 1950.5, but they described the two-month small-landlord exception instead of the general one-month rule. Q5 retrieved loosely related rent chunks (scores about 0.49), and Q6 retrieved nothing relevant (scores about 0.10).

**Which context changes helped.** The biggest gains came from the grounding rules and the relevance filter. No-RAG hallucinated on three questions: "30 days" for Q1, an invented "$10 per month or 5%" late-fee rule for Q5, and "Canberra" for Q6, which is true but not grounded. Basic RAG fixed Q1 but never used the exact refusal sentence. Context RAG got Q1, Q3, Q5 and Q6 right, cited source numbers in every answer (6/6 format compliance), and refused Q5 and Q6 with the exact text. Q6 was refused by the 0.35 relevance gate before the model was called. Q5 passed the gate and was refused by the model itself because of the grounding rule. De-duplication never triggered in this run: the character windows with a 50-character overlap did not produce near-copies in the top-k. Source labels and ordering made the citations checkable, for example "[1]" in Q1 pointing to chunk #025.

**Effect of k.** More context was not always better. For Q2, k = 1 and k = 3 led to a refusal because the second fact was missing, while k = 5 brought in #031 and the model answered both parts with citations. For Q3, k = 1 gave the cleanest answer, k = 3 added a hedge, and k = 5 added two just-cause chunks that pushed the model into refusing. For Q1 every k worked. So the best k depended on the question: small k for single-chunk facts and larger k for multi-chunk answers.

**Ambiguity and failures.** Q4 was not handled well. Both RAG configs stated "two months' rent" as the limit without saying it is an exception or asking what kind of charge was meant. The answer was grounded in the retrieved text but misleading. Context RAG's refusal on Q2 at k = 3 was faithful but unhelpful.

**Overall.** Retrieval quality set the ceiling: when the right chunk was missing (Q2 at k = 3, Q4), no prompt could fix the answer. Context quality decided whether the model stayed on topic, and the grounding prompt turned weak evidence into refusals instead of hallucinations. Context RAG was the only configuration that never hallucinated. Its remaining errors were cautious refusals and an unflagged ambiguity, not invented facts.
